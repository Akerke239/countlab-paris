Formula reference

Review the rules before taking the quiz.

Counting principle
👕👕👕 × 👖👖👖👖

Multiply independent choices

m × n

If you have 3 shirts and 4 pairs of trousers, there are 3 × 4 = 12 outfits.

Factorial

Arrange all objects

n! = n(n−1)…1

Four different books can be ordered in 4! = 24 ways.

Permutation

Order matters

P(n,r) = n!/(n−r)!

Assigning president and vice-president from 5 people: 5 × 4 = 20.

Combination

Order does not matter

C(n,r) = n!/[r!(n−r)!]

Choosing 2 students from 5: C(5,2) = 10 groups.

Classical probability
⚀ ⚁ ⚂ ⚃ ⚄ ⚅

Count favorable outcomes

P(E) = favorable / total

For a fair die, P(even) = 3/6 = 1/2.

Independent events
🪙 × 🪙

Multiply probabilities

P(A and B) = P(A)P(B)

Two independent fair coin tosses: P(HH) = 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4.

How to choose a method

Order matters? Use permutations. Order does not matter? Use combinations. Several stages? Multiply the number of choices. Probability? Identify equally likely outcomes and count favorable ones.
